# Cross-tradition visual grammar assessment — 003/004

## Foundational contrast
| Operation | Manichaeism | Catholicism |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Two independently existing primordial realms | One creator and a contingent created world |
| Evil | A primordial active principle of Darkness | Not a coeternal rival; deprivation/disorder in good created reality |
| Human predicament | Living light imprisoned in matter | Sin, suffering, and need for grace in a good but fallen world |
| Deliverance | Separation of light from matter | Salvation in Christ, including ultimate renewal of embodied creation |

## Reuse candidate
Distinct bounded identities, persistent relations, rupture, and structural change can be expressed by common operations. A viewer may not reliably decode these without a key; no comprehension experiment was run.

## Necessary distinctions
The same color does not imply the same conceptual type. Manichaean captive *light* represents a mythic cosmic reality; Catholic *goodness* is not a light-substance transferred from God. Showing creation *ex nihilo* as a beam extracted from God would incorrectly imply emanation; showing Catholic salvation as light escaping matter would imply Manichaeism.

## Art direction and exceptions
The cross is a conventional, recognizable reference to Christ. Colors, glow, stars, timing, spacing, and camera remain artistic decisions. They are not grammar rules unless tested and explicitly promoted.

## Failure modes to test
1. Caption dependence: can users decode the mechanisms without textual prompts?
2. Color aliasing: do viewers conflate the two metaphysical systems because both use light?
3. Motion aliasing: do viewers confuse Catholic creation with material transfer or Manichaean release with simple ascension?
4. Theological compression: does damaged relational topology adequately convey *privation* but oversimplify free choice, suffering, providence, and grace?
5. Finality: does the distinct ending clearly distinguish release/separation from renewal?

**Verdict:** promising comparative scenes, not a proven generative notation.
