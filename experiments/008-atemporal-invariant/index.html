Visual Reasoning Pilot 008 · Calibration case

Something changes. The relation does not.

Drag the triangle’s upper vertex or change its coordinates. This changes which triangle you are looking at, not the mathematical theorem that every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle has a 180° angle sum.

Manipulable construction

Always the same relation

Three angle measures vary together as you manipulate the triangle.

Static baseline: in Euclidean geometry, the interior angles of a nondegenerate triangle sum to 180°. A conventional static proof could place the three angles together along a straight line.

Three clocks, one test. Domain: timeless theorem Representation: triangle instance changes Exploration: dragging occurs in time

This is a calibration example, not a metaphysical film. It asks whether continuous manipulation helps reveal an invariant that a static explanation alone may leave abstract. We have not shown that it communicates an atemporal theological relationship, nor that it outperforms the static baseline for unfamiliar viewers.

Thinking prompt: If the form changes but its angle-sum does not, what is the visual evidence that the constant relationship was not produced by the movement? What additional visual convention would distinguish an eternal grounding relation from a historical causal event?
