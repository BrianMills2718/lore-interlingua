# Visual Thinking Study 005 — Sustaining Being

> **Historical/provisional record.** This file retains earlier examples and proposed next steps; current project decisions are in [Project Charter](../../docs/PROJECT_CHARTER.md), [Current State](../../docs/PROJECT_STATUS.md), and [Experiment Registry](../../docs/EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.md). Do not treat a past "gate" as a program-level stop rule.


**Status:** Exploratory art/research, not a finished theology film or an approved visual-notation rule.

## Working question

How might Catholic creation **ex nihilo** (creation without pre-existing material) and ongoing divine sustenance be represented without implying that God transfers a substance into the world?

We deliberately **hold the conceptual question fixed and vary the artistic expression**. Three sketches run synchronously in a self-contained interactive HTML workbench. The user can play, pause, scrub the timeline, and toggle names and interpretation notes.

## Three visual hypotheses

| Sketch | What it helps us think | What it risks implying |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A · The Page** | Free creation as an intelligible world's emergence and unfolding | The blank page already existed; God resembles a human artisan; creation happened only in the past |
| **B · Illumination** | Continual support without visible source depletion | Being means being visible; God is a physical light above the world |
| **C · The Held Chord** | Persistent supporting condition and distinct creaturely activity | God is a literal vibration; creaturely actions are mechanically synchronized |

The tree is a recognizable creature, not a member of the Trinity. None of the graphics claim that God is literally a spatial light, a writer, or a sound.

## Sources

Primary grounding: [Catechism of the Catholic Church §§293–302](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_4_the_creator.index.html), especially §§295–296 (free creation, no pre-existing material or emanation of divine substance), §300 (transcendence and presence), §301 (continuous sustenance and creaturely action), §302 (created order in development).

## Experiment protocol

1. Open [index.html](index.html) locally in a browser; no build or dependencies.
2. Observe the three representations with captions/labels visible.
3. Hide labels; explain what you think each animation asserts.
4. Scrub the 12-second loop to distinguish the initial appearance of the tree from its persistence and development.
5. Record conceptual insights and misreadings. Compare the usefulness of the metaphors rather than selecting a 'winner' based solely on aesthetic appeal.

**A new question produced by a visualization counts as research value**, even if no candidate has adequate communicative fidelity. Immediate viewer comprehension is not a prerequisite for private creative exploration; it is a later criterion for public explanatory work.

## Provisional findings

- A continuing relation is hard to depict without implying a physical force or transport process.
- Illustrating a creator as an object in the scene tends to spatialize divinity.
- A genuine creaturely process (growth) can be depicted independently of how divine dependence is signified.
- Captions can clarify metaphysical distinctions without making this 'just PowerPoint.'
- None of these sketches has demonstrated semantic correctness or audience comprehension; **promoted visual grammar rules: zero**.

## Next questions

- What metaphor can communicate dependence without the idea of an energy pipe or physical support?
- Can continuous ontological grounding coexist visually with autonomous creaturely action?
- Which ambiguities are fruitful provocations, and which contradict the tradition?
- How would a less literal, more lyrical work perform as a thinking instrument?

## Format decision after critique — 2026-10-27

This three-way interactive workbench is an **internal visual-thinking study**, not a model for an audience-facing lore film. It tests three possible visualizations of a *single selected Catholic doctrinal claim*. Future lore videos should follow one coherent tradition-specific story, rather than presenting alternative visual treatments as if they were competing theologies or combining incompatible accounts. Studies like this are valid research artifacts; a published film should choose and develop a narratively consistent interpretation with clearly scoped primary sources.
