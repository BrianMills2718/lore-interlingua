LORE INTERLINGUA / VISUAL THINKING STUDY 005

How can existence be sustained?

Three simultaneous sketches of one proposition from Catholic theology: God freely brings creatures into being and continues to sustain them, without transferring part of the divine substance. They are exploratory artistic metaphors, not literal depictions of God or definitive explanations of doctrine.

0.0 / 12s
HYPOTHESIS A

The Page

An intelligible world appears and changes without taking material from its author.

What it lets us think

Creation as intelligible and freely given; creatures have a story and their own unfolding.

What it may wrongly imply

God is a human artisan; the blank page already existed; creation is merely an event in past time.

HYPOTHESIS B

Illumination

The world has persistent visibility or intelligibility through a source that does not diminish.

What it lets us think

Dependence is continual, not merely an initial act. The created world remains distinguishable from its source.

What it may wrongly imply

Being is simply being visible, or God is a physical light located above the world.

HYPOTHESIS C

The Held Chord

One sustained condition permits multiple creatures to develop their own changing patterns.

What it lets us think

Sustaining is compatible with creaturely activity. Source and creatures are not identical.

What it may wrongly imply

God is a vibration or sound; creaturely activity is mechanically synchronized to a divine frequency.

One claim; three hypotheses

All three hold fixed: free creation, no pre-existing material, no loss of divine substance, continuing dependence, and genuine creaturely activity. Each metaphor inevitably adds further suggestions. The research task is to notice those suggestions and decide which illuminate the concept and which deform it.

This is a sketchbook for conceptual exploration—not a notation standard, and not a comprehension test already passed.

Questions created by drawing

  1. How do we show sustaining without depicting a fluid or physical energy beam?
  2. Does emphasizing a source always make God look spatially located?
  3. Can a creature appear genuinely active without seeming independent of its source of existence?
  4. Which visual implications are interesting ambiguity, and which are unacceptable doctrinal distortion?
Sources: Catechism of the Catholic Church §§295–302, especially creation ex nihilo (§296), transcendence and presence (§300), and continual sustenance (§301). Exploratory artwork, not official religious iconography. Scene time is pedagogical time, not a claimed chronology of God.
