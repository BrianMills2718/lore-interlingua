# Visual Thinking Animatic 007 — One Son, Two Natures

**Status:** exploratory moving storyboard, not a finished Catholic film or approved reusable visual grammar. [Open the interactive HTML](index.html) after downloading it; it runs offline without packages or external media.

## Working question

Can a dynamic visual language depict Catholic teaching that there is **one uncreated God in three distinct persons** and that the **same eternal Son assumes human nature** in history, without implying that the Son begins to exist, that there are three gods, or that the incarnate Christ is two persons?

This study follows **one coherent Catholic account**, sourced to the Catechism, not a comparative survey of incompatible interpretations.

## Four continuous movements

1. **Eternal relation (0–10 s):** Father, Son, Spirit and one undivided divine nature; the figures remain and never perform a chronological birth.
2. **Historical register (10–20 s):** Created history enters a separate register without changing eternal divine relations.
3. **The Incarnation (20–30 s):** Mary and a human figure named Jesus Christ enter, with a consistent SON identity and a human-nature boundary.
4. **One person, two natures (30–40 s):** Hold the same identity across both registers while distinguishing divine and human natures.

These are changes of **pedagogical attention**, not proofs that God occupies physical space. The Son's eternal begetting is not depicted as an event in time.

## Represent → manipulate → discover

- **Represent:** An artistic visual relation among three persons and one divine nature, plus the temporal assumption of humanity.
- **Manipulate:** Toggle off the **identity bridge**, the **nature layers**, or the **question overlay**; scrub through the transformation. The doctrines do not change when a graphic layer is hidden.
- **Discover:** Does two-position rendering imply two Sons? Does enclosure imply three fractions of God? Does cyan human nature imply mere costume? Would traditional iconography communicate the relation better?

A discovered problem is a **research result**, not proof that a metaphysical claim is false. An attractive visual transformation is not automatically a licensed deduction.

## Semantic audit

| Visual element | Intended correspondence | Obvious risk |
|---|---|---|
| Father / Son / Spirit, explicitly named | Distinct persons of one God | Three spatially separated deities |
| Single enclosing oval | One undivided nature | Three subdivisions of divinity |
| Same SON name/seal across registers | One continuous personal identity | Two Sons, one above and one below |
| Human figure with human-nature outline | True humanity assumed by the Son | Costume rather than complete nature |
| Mary | Mother of incarnate Jesus according to humanity | Generic figure lacking historical/sacramental context |
| Historical timeline beneath relation | Incarnation occurs in history; eternal relation does not | Implies divine reality literally spatially above the world |

The depiction remains intentionally schematic. Ordinary viewers need not learn a visual notation beforehand; the integrated labels do real communicative work.

## Testing

The self-contained HTML rendered in headless Chromium without JavaScript exceptions. Play/pause, timeline, identity bridge, nature layer and inquiry toggle were exercised. Screenshots and a 20-second accelerated preview are supporting media. No viewer comprehension or formal theological sign-off has been completed.

## Next decision

The next iteration should not merely add ornament. It should examine whether a **Catholic-iconographic alternative** can preserve the same theological distinctions and improve meaning without schematic duplicate figures. The project should return to the [Catholic world model](../../research/catholic-world-model/README.md) rather than copying an unreviewed drawing into a production film.
