# Film 006 — The World That Is Continually Created

**Status:** Implemented artistic interpretation / not an approved general visual-grammar convention.
**Tradition:** Catholicism, specifically the *Catechism of the Catholic Church* §§290–302 and 306–308.
**Runtime:** 84 seconds, 960×540 at 20 fps.
**Medium:** Continuous procedural 2D animation, cinematic framing, integrated onscreen language, original ambient score.
**Source:** https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/paragraph_4_the_creator.index.html

## The guiding question

How can every creature wholly depend on the Creator for its being and yet be a genuine cause of change within creation?

This is one coherent Catholic theological account, not a comparison to Manichaeism, Deism, or Gnosticism and not three artistic alternatives presented as different doctrines. The film explores **creation, conservation in being, and secondary causation**.

## Doctrinal constraints

1. God is uncreated; the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are not three created entities (§§290–292).
2. Creation is free, out of nothing, and not the transfer of divine stuff (§§295–296).
3. Creatures are real, good, and can act within creation (§§299, 306–308).
4. The Creator is not merely an initial maker who abandons the world (§301).
5. Divine conservation does not erase the legitimate action of creatures (§§301, 306–308).
6. Providence leads creation toward its ultimate end (§302), but this single film does not try to narrate all Catholic salvation history.

The title is **poetic**, describing continuous dependence, not an assertion that God re-performs an initial physical Big Bang at each instant.

## Visual story

The whole animation inhabits one continuous environment: an illustrated tree, water and stream, sunlight, soil, rain, and a human caretaker. There are no arbitrary numbered circles, no slide-by-slide metaphysical fact lists, and no depictions of God as a physical orb, giant hand, energy beam, or external material source.

1. **00–12 s — A living order.** Enter a living world already in activity. Its physical character is recognizable without a prior symbol lecture.
2. **12–26 s — Causes within creation.** Rainwater penetrates the soil, sunlight reaches leaves, and camera motion traces local interactions.
3. **26–40 s — Creaturely action.** A person waters the tree. A temporary blue moving notation tracks a water-related physical process near the roots; the camera moves closer to the same scene rather than changing slide.
4. **40–56 s — A new level of explanation.** The live scene persists while an additional, distributed fine contour develops across created structures. This artistic convention is intended to draw attention to *existence of the whole world*, not a traveling divine substance or a newly arriving physical force. Short text explicitly distinguishes this ontological question from material transfer.
5. **56–73 s — Sustained and acting.** The tree, water, weather, and caretaker continue moving while the thin contour persists. The Catholic claim of continued sustenance is presented alongside the reality of creaturely causes.
6. **73–84 s — Unfinished created life.** Fruit appears as the tree continues to develop. The film ends within the same inhabited world, without suggesting physical cosmic closure.

## Communicative choices

- **Recognizable imagery:** tree, person, roots, rain, stream, sunlight.
- **Motion:** rain, wind and leaves, water within creation, growth, human action, camera movement, and a separately coded continual contour.
- **Text:** only for proper doctrinal concepts and distinctions that cannot responsibly be conveyed through moving marks alone.
- **Ornament:** engraved botanical style, atmospheric colors, sunlight rings, background textures, and an original ambient score.
- **Language acquisition:** none required. Viewers can follow the physical story before interpreting the subtler ontological overlay.

## Semiotic / philosophical audit

The distributed golden contour is **an artistic hypothesis**, not a literal depiction of God or a promoted convention. It could still be mistaken for an energy field, or for divine presence becoming active only halfway through the story. The film's text describes the change as a shift in explanatory attention and says that existence is not another flowing substance. Further tests are needed before adopting the motif as a stable grammar rule.

The water particles around the roots are *physical causal flow*, not evidence that all religious processes are particle transfer. This distinction comes directly from the failure of earlier experiments to distinguish Manichaean light, Bailey's rays, Plotinian grounding and Gurdjieff's nourishment.

The person’s watering action **depicts visible deliberative behavior only**; it does not purport to settle free will or the agent’s mental state. Human action appears because Catholic secondary causation includes responsible human cooperation.

A captioned work remains partly verbal. That is intentional. This is **multimodal communication**, not a campaign to eliminate text or a proof that formal grammar can generate an artistic work without human interpretation.

## Test / acceptance

- Film narrates one source-scoped Catholic conception, not a comparison.
- Film contains a recognizable continuous world; changes in shot scale use camera motion, not blank cut slides.
- No arbitrary circles or nodes without meanings.
- Creator is not depicted as a material transmitter or person in the scene.
- Physical flow, agency and sustaining dependence are distinguishable using context plus text.
- Title, doctrinal propositions and source references are included in supporting docs.
- Local render, video and audio track are confirmed with ffprobe; representative frames inspected.
- All promoted visual conventions **remain provisional** until tested with viewers and cross-tradition cases.

## Remaining questions

1. Is the thin contour an intelligible metaphor for grounding, or a misleading 'God-field'?
2. Can the transition from physical causal explanation to ontological explanation work without having to tell the viewer they are different?
3. Does the viewer understand *continued dependence* without inferring that God is an additional physical cause?
4. Are the human/tree representations artistically rich enough for the long-term visual corpus, or should later work add more elaborate character and natural-history illustrations?
5. Would spoken narration make a substantially better public experience than the restrained written captions?
