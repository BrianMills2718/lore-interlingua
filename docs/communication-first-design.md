# Communication-first artistic design

**Status:** Working project principle — established in the 2026-10-27 design review. Applies to all visual experiments.

## Objective

The objective is **effective communication of complex philosophical, metaphysical, and esoteric systems**, not elimination of text and not complete automation of artistic design.

The project creates a **multimodal explanatory language**: a developing combination of compositional spatial/temporal notation, iconic images, learned symbols, verbal narration, written text, and artistic composition. Ontology, semantics, mathematics, logic, and linguistics support design reasoning; they do not uniquely determine the finished artwork.

> Prefer the medium or combination of media that best communicates the intended meaning. The visual grammar is an artistic instrument, not a constraint to be obeyed at the expense of understanding.

## One coherent lore per public film

**Initial production rule:** Every audience-facing lore film should depict **one coherent, internally articulated cosmological or theological account**. Do not build a film as a survey of competing beliefs, a list of interpretations, or a contrast-first definition of one tradition against another. Show the world *from within* the selected account, including its own entities, processes, relations, changes, and narrative logic.

- **Scope the account before production.** State a named tradition and, where appropriate, a specific text, school, period, or doctrinal strand. Broad labels such as "Christianity" and "Gnosticism" contain substantive internal disagreements; coherence must not be fabricated by quietly combining incompatible sources.
- **One explanatory world.** A film may show tensions and complexity *inside that account* when they belong to it, but should not silently cut to a different cosmology or alternate interpretation.
- **Artistic plurality is compatible with doctrinal unity.** The filmmakers may test many visual metaphors, vary style, use text and iconography, and make creative decisions. Those are multiple *rendering approaches*, not multiple competing worldviews in the same finished film.
- **Research comparisons remain backstage.** Cross-tradition comparisons and three-way visual thinking studies are useful working documents, optional companion material, or possible later formats; they are not the default narrative structure of an individual film.
- **Provenance is still maintained.** Document the selected source(s) and interpretive choices in production notes. The public film can begin with one concise attribution/disclaimer, rather than constant qualifications.
- **Focused episodes over survey montages.** A single film may explore one mechanism deeply instead of trying to summarize an entire religion in 30 seconds.

**Release check:** Could a careful viewer identify whose cosmology is being represented, and would its major claims make sense together on the terms of that tradition? If not, narrow or revise the film.

## The role of text

Text is a first-class, legitimate communicative resource. It may be the best way to convey:

- proper names and unfamiliar terms (e.g., Father, Son, Holy Spirit; Sun Absolute);
- exact doctrinal distinctions and propositions with no compact visual encoding;
- semantic disambiguation of otherwise misleading geometry, causal arrows, or motion;
- concise narration and optional definitions during onboarding.

Use labels integrated with the scene, not automatically as a separate slide with an illustration. When a label identifies a node, anchor it clearly to that node and retain identity through subsequent transitions.

Avoid both extremes: **text avoidance** when it harms clarity, and **text substitution** where moving graphics merely decorate a paragraph instead of expressing the relation or transformation in question.

## Accumulating visual literacy across a large corpus

The project may eventually contain thousands of videos. A stable repertoire of visual constructions should yield **cumulative visual literacy**: recurring viewers can read more meaning from fewer labels because learned conventions transfer between works.

The corpus should support both:

1. **First encounters**: enough text, illustration, or a compact key for viewers who have not learned prior conventions.
2. **Returning viewers**: stable semantic mappings and compositional reuse, allowing more expressive, compressed visual sequences.

Do not assume viewers watch episodes in sequence. Prefer brief contextual onboarding and optional keys over mandatory prerequisite viewing.

A learned convention earns promotion only through repeated, legible use and eventually comprehension testing—not because it appears formally elegant.

## First-contact legibility and optional literacy education

**Do not make training a prerequisite to watching any episode.** A viewer arriving at episode 500 should still be able to grasp its central process or argument without first watching a separate tutorial about the notation. Regular viewers may perceive further detail and read denser combinations because conventions recur.

Design for **progressively discoverable meaning**:

1. **First encounter:** meaning should be reasonably inferable through iconic resemblance, perceptually motivated motion, composition, context, appropriately anchored labels, and narration. Give unfamiliar concepts enough on-scene scaffolding; avoid requiring prior viewing.
2. **Repeated encounter:** maintain stable conventions so viewers accumulate implicit familiarity, reducing explanatory overhead without sacrificing clarity for newcomers.
3. **Optional explicit instruction:** offer standalone episodes about the visual language, its design principles, and its notation. These deepen literacy and support advanced use, but are not required to follow ordinary lore episodes.

**Critical qualification:** no representation can be guaranteed intuitive to *everyone*, across cultural backgrounds, abilities, and unfamiliar doctrines. Some relations are too abstract for unambiguous depiction. In these cases use text, narration, examples, or a short local key without treating that choice as failure.

**Testing requirement:** use both first-time viewers with no notation training and returning viewers. Ask what they believe a sequence means before revealing the intended answer, and compare interpretations. Determine whether basic comprehension is achievable immediately and whether repeated exposure improves precision, speed, or recall. Do not assume exposure alone produces literacy.

The desired progression is **immediate narrative comprehension plus optional, accumulating technical fluency**, not a course that the audience must complete before understanding the stories.

## Two different success criteria: public communication and private exploration

**A public explanatory video** should aim to make a central idea intelligible to an unfamiliar viewer, while allowing advanced or difficult material to remain genuinely difficult. Perfect comprehension is not a realistic obligation, especially for contested philosophical claims.

**A visual-thinking study** can succeed even without immediate understandability. It may expose hidden assumptions, provoke new metaphors, encourage iterative reconceptualization, or bring previously unnoticed tensions into focus. Its immediate output can be a better question rather than a better explanation.

Repeated viewing and partial understanding are legitimate modes of learning. We are developing instruments for `groping toward understanding`, not a guaranteed decoding machine.

These modes share tools but do not share an identical quality metric. A private exploratory sketch should not fail just because it is not yet legible to a broad audience; a public explanatory video should not excuse ungrounded confusion as intrinsically profound.

## Selective visual metaphor: meaning is not literal identity

The project relies on **conceptual metaphor** (using a familiar perceptible phenomenon to explore another concept) and **analogical mapping** (carrying selected relations or properties from one domain into another). Literal similarity is neither expected nor required.

For example, an animation may depict **grace as water, light, fire, or an illuminating current** even though Catholic theology does not identify grace as a measurable physical substance. The visual treatment may communicate reception, renewal, transformation, spiritual participation, or a sustaining gift. It does **not** thereby assert mass, viscosity, conservation of fluid volume, divine depletion, or laboratory-testable mechanics.

This is not merely a concession to artistic license. Catholic tradition itself uses symbolically charged substances: the Catechism treats water as a sign of the Holy Spirit's action in Baptism (CCC §694), water as a symbol of grace (§1217), and baptismal washing as purification and new birth (§§1262–1266). Grace is discussed as an undeserved gift and participation in divine life (§§1996–2005). Primary sources:
- [CCC §§691–701, symbols of the Holy Spirit](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_one/section_two/chapter_three/article_8/ii_the_name%2C_titles%2C_and_symbols_of_the_holy_spirit.html)
- [CCC §§1217–1222, baptismal water](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/ii_baptism_in_the_economy_of_salvation.html)
- [CCC §§1262–1266, grace of Baptism](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_two/section_two/chapter_one/article_1/vii_the_grace_of_baptism.html)
- [CCC §§1996–2005, grace](https://www.vatican.va/content/catechism/en/part_three/section_one/chapter_three/article_2/ii_grace.html)

**Method:** Distinguish a metaphor's intended structural correspondences from the additional physical properties and associations of its imagery. We may identify some explicitly, but should **not** demand an exhaustive formal assignment for every impression, particle, color, or ornament. Evocative surplus (additional associations that emerge for viewers) may be productive.

**Critical judgment:** The relevant risk is not that a visual element *could* be read literally. The risk is that the work **prominently and persistently communicates a material doctrinal claim that contradicts its intended interpretation**, or replaces the tradition's own important distinctions with an alien mechanism. Establish the context through imagery, speech, or brief text rather than sterilizing metaphors.

**Design evaluation:** Ask what the metaphor reveals, what relations it invites viewers to explore, what it obscures, and whether the resulting insights or errors are productive. Do not reject an image just because its source phenomenon has properties the target lacks.

## Visual reasoning is bidirectional

The production system is both an expressive language **and a thinking instrument**: it can **represent** ideas, enable **manipulation and rule-bound or analogical reasoning**, and **reveal** gaps and implications that change our conceptual model. These functions feed one another. See [Visual reasoning as an instrument](visual-reasoning-instrument.md). Neither a legal graphic transformation nor an evocative analogy is automatically a theorem or a factual claim.

## Allocation of communicative work

| Mode | Often best for |
|---|---|
| Spatial structure | Part/whole, containment, relation, hierarchy, simultaneity |
| Temporal behavior | Recurrence, transformation, persistence, phase, response, directional influence |
| Iconic depiction | Recognizable persons, worlds, places, traditions, ceremonial objects |
| Written labels | Names, precise distinctions, short interpretations, necessary definitions |
| Spoken narration | Qualified claims, conceptual transitions, reasoning across scenes |
| Artistic treatment | Atmosphere, salience, emotional quality, continuity, evocation, metaphor |

These are affordances (useful tendencies), not exclusive allocations. Artistic judgment chooses among them.

## Worked example: Catholic theological imagery

Three circles by themselves are not an adequate depiction of the Trinity or of created reality. They invite multiple readings. If the aim is to represent the Trinity, it may be appropriate to name the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit** explicitly, use conventional Trinitarian iconography where helpful, and compose their distinction-within-unity carefully. Catholic theology does not regard these three divine persons as three creatures. If the topic is created entities, depict identifiable creatures or label them, rather than relying on arbitrary anonymous circles.

A film about divine creation and sustenance can combine continuing dynamic relations with labels such as *God*, *created order*, and *sustains*. The text does not invalidate the visual language; it stabilizes its meaning.

## Evaluation criteria

1. Can first-time viewers understand the mechanism or doctrine without mandatory prior training or a slide-deck-length explanation?
2. Do experienced viewers understand reused conventions with less verbal scaffolding?
3. Do visuals communicate information that static text or static diagrams would not express as clearly?
4. Where text remains, is it doing real communicative work rather than compensating for arbitrary visuals?
5. Does the work remain aesthetically engaging and internally faithful to the tradition, without claiming an involuntary or false equivalence to other traditions?

**Rejected metrics:** fewer words at any cost, percentage of the scene generated by formal code, and adherence to one visual style irrespective of the material.

## Relationship to the ongoing experiment

Visual Experiment 004 was criticized because it behaved like a presentation and used unlabeled abstract circles with unintended implications. The revision should not respond by banning text: it should choose a specific internal Catholic process, keep the dynamic relation visually intelligible, and use text or recognizable imagery where they communicate more effectively.

The project retains a single opening disclaimer/attribution when presenting a lore narrative on its own terms; this is distinct from whether ordinary explanatory text belongs inside a scene.
