# Visual reasoning as an artistic and cognitive instrument

**Status:** Project method, 2026-10-27 design conversation. This document refines [communication-first design](communication-first-design.md) and the [Catholic world model](../research/catholic-world-model/README.md). It does not introduce a metaphysical claim or an approved universal visual grammar.

## The objective

We are developing an **artistic visual language for representing, thinking with, and discovering conceptual structure** in coherent philosophical/esoteric/religious lore.

The meaning of "reasoning" here is broader than calculating a proof. Visualizations can support at least three distinct but mutually reinforcing operations:

| Function | What happens | Example question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Represent / externalize** | Translate a selected lore's entities, relations, properties and transformations into visible, audible and textual form | How might "one divine nature and three persons" be presented without depicting three gods? |
| **Manipulate / reason** | Operate on a representation: preserve identity while changing a relation, change a viewpoint, compose structures, compare a before/after state, or test implications under stated rules | What changes if one nature is assumed by an already-existing person? What *does not* change? |
| **Discover / inquire** | Notice a gap, ambiguity, analogy, contradiction, constraint or unexplored possibility raised by construction/manipulation; feed it back into research and visual design | Did our animation accidentally suggest that the Son first exists at the Incarnation? |

These form a **feedback loop**, not a one-way semantic-to-image pipeline:

```text
tradition-specific sources and interpretations
          |                       ^
          v                       |
conceptual interlingua <-- new questions / distinctions
          |                       ^
          v                       |
visual works <--> manipulation / exploration
          |
          +--> audience communication / narrative experience
```

**Provenance of idea:** The conversation began with a user goal of representing complex lore in a reusable visual language. The assistant proposed that visualization can be an *instrument of thought* rather than only an output representation. The user developed the proposal further by emphasizing bidirectional or three-way representation, computing/manipulating, and revealing/discovering. Neither contribution alone is a claim of a novel theory.

## Not all visual moves are valid inference

Separate three modes in our notes, not necessarily as intrusive on-screen labels:

- **Rule-governed manipulation:** A transformation follows explicitly specified semantics and permitted operations. If the formal model is sound, the resulting depiction is a candidate consequence of that model.
- **Analogical exploration:** A metaphor suggests structural similarities or possibilities. Its apparent consequences are *hypotheses*, not deductions. They may prompt new source reading or modeling.
- **Aesthetic evocation:** Composition, atmosphere, shape, motion, text, voice, and symbol create associations that may advance understanding without a testable formal inference.

A formally legal transformation can still be philosophically misleading, and a nonformal image can be revelatory. Do not equate machine validation with truth or comprehension.

## A creative research cycle

1. Select a **specific coherent lore account**, with primary-source boundaries and positive internal claims.
2. Extract a small semantic problem with important identities, relations, constraints and possible changes.
3. Explore artistically using dynamic geometry, recognizable imagery, traditional symbols, motion, speech, and text. Artistic liberty is not an exception to the method.
4. Manipulate the visual representation, including deliberately varying assumptions or vantage points as a **research exercise** (not automatically a claim inside the lore).
5. Record what the visual experiment makes thinkable: useful correspondence, tension, contradictory inference, unexpected question, representation gap.
6. Check source fidelity when a visual insight purports to be a claim about a tradition. Revise semantics and the visual vocabulary as needed.
7. Make an internally coherent public film **after** resolving which exploratory hypotheses it is depicting. Preserve separate backstage studies for competing visual hypotheses.

## Roles of metaphor and notation

A metaphor maps **selected** properties of a visible phenomenon to an abstract concept. Representing grace as water can evoke gift, reception, renewal, purification, and transformation; it does not assert viscosity, finite volume, or physical transport. Emerging associations are not automatically errors. Reject a choice when its dominant reading contradicts the specific lore, not because metaphor is nonliteral.

Our formal notation is an **instrument**, not a constraint that preempts story, theology, or art. Existing symbols, icons, characters and words can be used. Reusable visual conventions are valuable where earned by repeated useful instances, but neither visual purity nor full auto-generation is a success metric.

## Evaluation is purpose-relative

| Artifact | Success evidence |
| --- | --- |
| A private visual-thinking study | It exposes a useful question, constraint, alternative structure or conceptual tension, even without immediate readability |
| A public lore film | Its specific theological subjects and relations are coherent, audience-facing, artistically worthwhile, and reasonably accessible without prerequisite language lessons |
| A reusable notation rule | Readers can learn it and apply it to novel cases; its conventions survive cross-domain transfer without losing important meaning |
| A formal/algorithmic manipulation | Its claimed inference preserves the stated model semantics under documented rules |

**Do not claim** that viewers automatically learn from repetition, that every image computes a theorem, or that the existence of a render is evidence of metaphysical truth.

## First stress test — Trinity and Incarnation

- **Representation:** one uncreated divine nature, three distinct divine persons; the Son retains divine identity throughout.
- **Manipulation:** introduce a historical human nature related to the **same Son**, not a new deity, new divine nature or second person.
- **Discovery:** inspect which visual moves create false temporal origin, multiple gods, a person/nature swap, or two-person Christology.
- **Next artifact:** one limited, interactive animatic and a design audit; **not** a finished Catholicism overview.
