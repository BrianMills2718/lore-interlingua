# Semantic casting and first-film options

**Status:** Preproduction proposal, not an approved storyboard or an instruction to render. Read [the world model](README.md) first. We are now asking what is worth depicting, *before* choosing attractive scenery.

## One film, one positive Catholic account

We propose **a Catholic account as expressed in the Nicene Creed and the Catechism**, not a list of disparate Christian interpretations or a film defined primarily by differences from Manichaeism.

The first film should reveal a theological **world**: its source of being, forms of creatures, divine/human relations, crisis, response, and final telos (end or purpose). A film need not claim to exhaust the tradition.

## Casting contract: key visual subjects

| Subject | Theological identity and necessary property | Possible evocative treatment | Principal visual misreading to avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| One God | Uncreated, eternal, one undivided divine nature; not a physical object inside creation | Aniconic sacred space, integrated text, existing Catholic iconography interpreted with context | A large creature, a finite object, or fourth divine person |
| Father | Distinct divine person, eternally Father of the Son | Word 'Father'; relational sacred iconography | Chronological fatherhood or creator separate from Son/Spirit |
| Son / eternal Word | Distinct divine person, eternally begotten, uncreated | Unified recurring visual identity continuing into the Incarnation | Son begins to exist when Jesus is born |
| Holy Spirit | Distinct divine person, uncreated; gives life and sanctifies | Dove/flame motifs when narratively appropriate, optional name label | Spirit literally a bird, flame, or impersonal force |
| Trinity | Distinct persons, one divine nature, inseparable in divine action | Relational Trinitarian motif, perhaps labeled; sustained co-presence rather than three sequential births | Three gods, three parts of God, one person switching masks |
| Angels | Spiritual created intelligences with will; divine messengers | Catholic angelic art and movement serving specific events | All angels are winged biological humans, or demons |
| Satan / fallen angels | Created angels rejecting God, not coeternal divine principles | Fall as a changed will/relationship rather than creation of an evil substance | A second ultimate cosmic source |
| Material cosmos | Good created reality capable of development and real secondary causation | Natural history, heavens, material texture and living processes | Matter intrinsically evil, or creation as portion of divine substance |
| Human persons | Unity of body and spiritual soul, rational freedom and capacity for relation with God | Embodied human figures with agency and recognizable experience | Persons as purely ghosts imprisoned in a body |
| Adam and Eve | First parents as understood by Catholic original-sin doctrine; Genesis uses figurative storytelling | Biblical narrative iconography carefully labeled as scriptural imagery | Unqualified documentary video of prehistory |
| Mary | Created human mother of Jesus, Theotokos because her son is the one divine person of the Son | Annunciation, consent, Nativity; recognizable art | Mary is divine or originates the Son's eternal nature |
| Jesus Christ | One eternal divine person with full divine and human natures | A **single continuous figure/identity** through human history, cross, resurrection | Two characters (human Jesus + divine Son), or a merely human messenger |
| Cross and Resurrection | Historical saving center in Catholic proclamation | Cross, burial, empty tomb and risen Christ; connected transformation of relation | Mere death or physical resuscitation detached from salvation |
| Church | Body of Christ, People of God, Temple of Spirit | Multiple embodied people joined in sacrament and communal life | A building as the entire Church, or a second divine person |
| Sacraments | Efficacious signs entrusted to the Church | Specific ritual actions using water, bread/wine, anointing, words | Every puddle means Baptism; every blue particle means grace |
| Grace | Unmerited divine gift and participation in divine life; heals/elevates persons | **Water, light, fire, illumination, renewal**, or changed relationship may be deliberately evocative metaphors; Catholic sacramental symbolism already uses material signs | Do not mistake metaphorical water/light for a literal finite reservoir or mechanically depleted divine resource |
| Heaven / purgatory / hell | Distinct postmortem conditions and ultimate destinies, not straightforward astronomical coordinates | Theological art, text, attention to communion/purification/separation | Three planets at different altitudes |
| Final resurrection / new creation | Bodies and cosmos renewed in Christ | Recognizable embodied continuity with transformed creation | Permanent escape of pure souls from matter |

Decorative features remain free. But **foreground subjects, repeated emblems, and central transformations require defined semantic roles** and an audit of unintended meaning.

## Candidate narrative routes

### A. Main recommendation — *The Catholic Cosmos: From the Trinity to New Creation*

**Format:** A deliberately provisional 6–9-minute artistic introductory film. One coherent account framed by the Creed. Structured in seven connected movements, with repeated motifs that *actually refer to Catholic concepts*. Not seven title slides.

1. **Who God is.** One God, three distinct divine persons, eternal relations; introduction to divine personal identity without a sequence of three births.
2. **Creation.** One free Trinitarian act; spiritual/material world; humans as embodied spiritual persons. Establish difference between created and uncreated existence.
3. **The drama of freedom.** Good created order, angelic rebellion, human sin and wounded communion—darkness is not a second creator.
4. **Covenant and promise.** God acting within human history and Israel's story. Positive narrative bridge, not a theological detour.
5. **Incarnation and redemption.** Mary, the Holy Spirit, and the Son assuming human nature; then Christ's life, cross and Resurrection. Maintain continuous identity of the Son.
6. **Present participation.** Church, Spirit, grace and sacraments, with specifically marked Baptism and Eucharist.
7. **Final fulfillment.** Judgment, resurrection of the body, communion with God, and renewal of creation in Christ. Distinguish continuing embodied fulfillment from a mere flight out of matter.

**Benefits:** The viewer encounters the central metaphysical actors and relations before they are dramatized. Grounded in creed, sufficiently rich to support later thematic films. **Risk:** A 6–9-minute overview may still be too compressed; treat it as orientation, not full theology.

### B. High-depth pilot — *The Trinity and the Incarnation*

**Format:** 3–5 minutes on one profound relation, potentially more philosophically productive for our visual-language research.

Start with one divine nature/three persons (atemporal), then the Son's historical Incarnation (temporal), without multiplying divine persons. Continue through Christ's two natures and human agency. This is a stronger semantic-stress test and less dependent on arbitrary scenery.

**Benefit:** Forced to solve genuinely complex ontology: identity, relation, person/nature, eternity/history. **Risk:** Requires careful text and iconography and may remain difficult to grasp after multiple viewings.

### C. Focused art film — *The New Creation*

**Format:** 3–5 minutes. From embodied human existence through sin, Christ's Resurrection and final transformation of cosmos and body.

**Benefit:** Rich temporal visual development and recognizable narrative. **Risk:** Would need context to avoid making Catholic theology look like a generic mythology of death and rebirth.

**Recommendation:** Route A for the first **representative** Catholic overview, preceded by a small conceptual test for the person/nature and eternal/historical distinctions in Route B. Neither requires producing a film in this PR.

## Communication and visual-language plan

- **Source terminology stays visible.** Labels like *Father*, *Son*, *Holy Spirit*, *human nature*, *grace*, *Incarnation* are allowed; no pressure to replace precise names with circles.
- **Dynamic semantics should bear real work.** Relations within the Trinity should persist; creation is a free origin, the Incarnation is assumption of human nature by the **same** Son, sin changes moral relation, sacraments are specific ritual encounters, resurrection transforms embodied existence.
- **Conventional art is welcome.** Cross, Annunciation, Pentecost flame/dove, Eucharistic bread and wine are meaningfully grounded symbols, not violations of a pure generative grammar.
- **Formalism is a production aid.** We use relation/identity constraints to spot bad interpretations and support iterative visual thinking; final composition remains art-directed.
- **Narrative immersion.** One opening framing statement about this being a visual interpretation of Catholic teaching; avoid recurring disclaimers or constant comparison to other traditions.
- **First-contact and repeated literacy.** The work must stand alone, with optional deeper explanation of reusable visual structures outside the film.

## Pre-production review questions (must be answered before rendering)

1. Does our audience-facing film aim to introduce **Catholic lore in breadth** (Route A) or test the deep representational challenge of **divine identity and Incarnation** (Route B)?
2. Do the depicted divine persons have a legible, source-consistent relationship? What makes 'one nature, three persons' visually different from 'three gods'?
3. Can viewers identify every foreground actor or symbol, and can we explain why each belongs in this lore?
4. Does the film reflect the distinctive narrative **Christ at the center**, or still frame Catholicism as generic nature metaphysics?
5. Which transitions are historical versus eternal? Does film time imply false chronology?
6. Can we test an early 15–30-second **animatic** (rough moving storyboard) before investing in full rendering?
7. What ambiguities help viewers explore deeper meaning, and which misunderstandings become central distortions rather than ordinary metaphorical properties?
